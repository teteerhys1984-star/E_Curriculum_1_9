Grammar 1 — Possessive ’s

Owner + ’s + noun

Lara’s uncle · Charles’s mother · Molly’s doll · my aunt’s house

For a singular owner, add ’s to show possession or a relationship. The owner comes before the thing owned: “Molly’s doll” and “my aunt’s house.”

Grammar 2 — Plural possessive apostrophe

students’ books · pupils’ uniform · giraffes’ necks

For a regular plural ending in s, put the apostrophe after the s: students’. For an irregular plural that does not end in s, add ’s: children’s toys, women’s bags. Therefore, write children’s, not childrens’.

Useful conversation

Tell me about your family.

This phrase invites someone to describe their family.

How many brothers and sisters have you got?

This question asks about the number of brothers and sisters someone has.

I’ve got one brother and two sisters.

This is an example response giving the number of siblings.

I come from a small / big family.

This phrase describes whether someone’s family is small or large.
