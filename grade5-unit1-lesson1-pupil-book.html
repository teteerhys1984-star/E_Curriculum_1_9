LEARNING GOALS

1. School places

Learn the names of places at school. “Where do you have science?” asks where a lesson takes place.

Model from the book
Where do you have science?
I have science in the science lab.

Useful pattern: in the + place — in the classroom / in the library / in the computer lab.

2. School rules

have to expresses obligation: something you must do. don't have to means it is not necessary. It does not mean “must not”.

Use I / we / you / they have to, but he / she / it has to.

Example: Don’t run in the corridor; you have to walk.

Meaning: لا تركض في الممر؛ يجب أن تمشي. “have to” = يجب أن، و“don’t” هنا نهي عن الركض.

3. Questions and negatives

  • Do we have to wear a school uniform? — Yes, we do. / No, we don’t.
  • Does she have to wear a school uniform? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn’t.
  • He doesn’t have to bring a sandwich. = ليس من الضروري أن يحضر شطيرة.

After does / doesn’t, use the base form have, not “has”: Does she have to…?

4. School stages and reading

The textbook chart lists Nursery (KG 1–3), Basic/Primary (Grades 1–9), and Secondary (Grades 10–12). The reading says British children start primary school at five and secondary at eleven; in Syria, basic school starts at six and secondary at fifteen. Both systems require special exams for university, according to the passage.

Reading check

At what age do children start basic school in Syria?

According to the passage: at the age of six.

5. Pronunciation: silent h

The book groups what, when, why and hour, honest, honour under “Silent h”. The written page alone does not provide the audio, so listen to a reliable model pronunciation rather than guessing from spelling.
