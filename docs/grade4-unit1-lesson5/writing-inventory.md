## Classification rule

A printed activity counts as **Writing** only where the book requires the pupil to produce a
written response. Matching, ticking/crossing, circling/choosing, listening-only, drawing and
oral pair work are listed as excluded, with the reason.

### `pb-10` corrections — read from the printed underlining

The scanned underlining marks exactly one word per sentence; the correction follows from the
grades 3–4 grammar already taught in this unit (present simple agreement, *be*, *have got*).

| # | Printed sentence | Underlined | Correction | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | There **is** two beds and a wardrobe in my bedroom. | *is* | *are* | Agreement with the plural subject |
| 2 | I like history but my friend **like** English. | *like* | *likes* | Third-person singular |
| 3 | Lily always **wake** up at 7 o'clock in the morning. | *wake* | *wakes* | Third-person singular |
| 4 | Dana **are** 12 years old. | *are* | *is* | Singular subject |
| 5 | Sally **is** brown eyes. | *is* | *has got* | Needs a repair beyond the printed word; **disclosed as the one item whose answer the source does not settle by a single-word substitution.** |

Item 5 is presented as an illustrative correction with that caveat, not as a unique key.

### `wb-7` — pictures read from the scan

Row 1: globe with compass and star (Geography) · microscope, flasks and atom (Science) · a
thought bubble reading **Arabic** over a child with balloons and Arabic letters (Arabic) ·
football, boxing glove, ball and trainer (Sports). Row 2: the word **ENGLISH** in a busy
collage (English) · a guidebook character with a Roman-style building (History) · sheet music
with a violin (Music) · an artist's palette and easel (Art). Every cue names a subject that is
printed somewhere in the same book, so the mapping is verifiable from the source.

### `wb-9` — why two of six items stay illustrative

Row 1: `ABCD` board → *English* (verifiable); world map → *Geography is* + an adjective the
book does not print → illustrative only; a still-life drawing board → *Art is* + an adjective,
again open. Row 2: geometry board → *I am … at maths* (the natural word, *good*, is taught in
the same unit); music stave → `……… is interesting` → *Music*; science board → *Science … useful*
→ *is*. Where the book prints no word bank and the blank is an adjective, the example is
labelled as an example, not as the answer.

### `wb-8` and `wb-12` — reproducible from the source

(*Lucy: My favourite subject is history.*), so the missing pieces are structural. `wb-12`'s four
pictures are unambiguous (a boy with a paintbrush, a house beside a tree, a boy waking up, two
children with maths symbols), but each line is free sentence writing, so several true sentences
fit; the models are labelled sample sentences.

## Correction made in this batch

The earlier Pupil's Book and Workbook pages had **only** five/six generic per-page study
textareas (`study-5-1` … `study-5-11`) and no per-exercise structure. Fifteen writing
activities were therefore never separately answerable, and no activity had its own model,
persistence, status or reset. That is what this batch replaces, while keeping the existing
`study-5-N` fields (see *Changes* below) so no pupil draft is lost.
