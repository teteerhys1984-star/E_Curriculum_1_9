# Source-by-source review — Grade 4, Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Reference and method

- The title printed on both books is **2 My Family**.
- The six supplied images are the authoritative source; no OCR text was used as a substitute for reading them.
- Images 1–3 are Pupil’s Book pages 6, 7 and 8. Images 4–6 are Workbook pages 8, 9 and 10. The two books are kept separate and in their original page order.
- The six uploads are preserved unchanged as `assets/grade4-unit1-lesson2/source/*.png`. Their SHA-256 hashes, sizes and dimensions are recorded in the [source manifest](../../assets/grade4-unit1-lesson2/source-manifest.json) and re-checked by `npm test`.
- No text has been re-typed from imagination: anything the images do not settle is listed under “Uncertain or incomplete” below and marked on the page.
- Vocabulary translations, English teaching notes, the test questions and the extra practice answers are supporting additions. They are labelled as such on every page.
- “Explicit” means the answer is printed in the image. “Inferred” means the answer follows from the printed grammar or from matching a printed item, where the book prints no key.

## Listening activities

Pupil’s Book activities 1 and 2 and Workbook exercises 3 and 4 need the official recordings. The recordings were not supplied. The instructions and items are kept exactly as printed, each page shows a visible note, no transcript or audio file was created, and none of these items is scored in the lesson test.

## Printed answers that are already in the source

Only two answers are printed in the six images: the tick in Pupil’s Book 7a item 1 and the red example sentence in Pupil’s Book 9a. Both are labelled “printed in the source”. Everything else marked as an answer on this site is an editorial suggestion and says so.

## Uncertain or incomplete

1. **Pupil’s Book activity 3, third bullet** is printed as `His ………………` with a dotted line. The book does not print the completed sentence and the picture alone does not fix which sentence the learner should write, so the page asks the learner to complete it and supplies no answer.
2. **Pupil’s Book activity 5** can be read more than one way: the top-right girl clearly fits “Long blond hair”, and the top-left boy clearly fits “Short black hair”, but the top-left boy also has brown eyes and the bottom-left woman has brown hair and brown eyes. The exercise is therefore kept as a classroom task, is not scored, and the ambiguity is stated on the page.
3. **Workbook exercise 1** mixes two things in one branch of the table: the left-hand box in the second row reads `His …………` above the name `Mary` (a relationship word, answered by the text “My mum Mary”), while the right-hand box reads `His father` above a dotted line (a name, answered by “My dad, James”). The transcription notes each blank type separately and does not merge the two.
4. **Workbook exercise 8** ends with two dotted lines that have no printed words beside them. They are reproduced as two unlabelled writing fields with an explanatory note, not as invented prompts.
5. **Workbook exercise 5** is a free description of a drawing. No answer is supplied, because several true sentences are possible.
6. **Workbook exercises 3 and 4** cannot be completed without the recording, so their answers are explicitly left absent.

All other wording in the six images was readable at full size and is transcribed without change.
