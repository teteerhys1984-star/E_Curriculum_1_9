Grammar 1 — Present Simple

Used for: facts which are always true; habits or things we do regularly. Adverbs printed: always, often usually, sometimes, never, everyday/week/year.

Examples printed: Water boils at 100 degrees. · I drink coffee every morning. · He doesn't drink coffee every morning. · Does water boil at 100 degrees?

We use the present simple for facts that are always true and for habits or repeated actions. With he, she, and it, add -s to the verb (for example, He drinks). Common frequency adverbs include always, usually, sometimes, never, and every day. Note: the printed list reads “often usually” without a comma and “everyday” as one word; these forms are preserved as printed.

Grammar 2 — Present Progressive

Used for: activities happening at the moment of speaking; temporary activities. Adverbs printed: now, at the moment these days, this week/ month/year.

Examples printed: The water is boiling now, so you can put in the pasta. · I'm drinking too much coffee these days because I'm so busy at work.

-ing spelling (printed models): play → playing · hide ( remove the “e”) → hiding · die ( put “y” instead of ie) → dying

We use the present progressive for actions happening now and temporary activities: am/is/are + verb-ing. The Workbook prints three -ing spelling models: play → playing; hide → hiding (drop the e); and die → dying (change ie to y). It does not print a rule about doubling the final consonant, so we do not attribute that rule to the book.

Grammar 3 — Future Tense (will / going to)

Printed uses — with will: to make decisions, promises, predictions; to state a fact about the future. With going to: to talk about an event planned before the time of speaking; to make a future prediction based on facts or evidence.

Examples printed: We will go to the theater tomorrow. · I will help you if I have enough time. · It's cloudy.I think it will rain. · Global warming will cause more deserts. · They are going to spend their vacation in Spain next year. · He is going to graduate next year. · Negative: We won't go… / He is not going to graduate… · Questions: Will you go…? / Is he going to graduate…?

Use will for decisions, promises, predictions, and statements about the future. Use going to for plans made before speaking and predictions based on evidence. The Workbook exercise gives this example: “He is going to spend his vacation in Lattakia next summer.” Unlike the other two tense tables, the printed future table has no adverbs box; “theater” retains the book’s US spelling.
