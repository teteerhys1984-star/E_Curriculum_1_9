Grammar notes

1. Advice with should and shouldn’t

Use should + the plain verb to give advice: You should get enough sleep. Use shouldn’t + the plain verb to say what is not a good idea: You shouldn’t spend a long time playing computer games. The verb never changes: should to get and shouldn’t spend a long time are not printed on the page.

2. Suggestions with Why don’t you …?

Why don’t you + the plain verb makes a friendly suggestion: Why don’t you walk for an hour every day? It is a question, so it ends with a question mark.

3. should with everyone

Everyone takes should, not shoulds: Everyone should drink lots of water every day.

4. Talking about quantity

How many asks about things you can count: How many hours do you sleep every night? How much asks about things you cannot count: How much do you walk every day? Both patterns are printed on Workbook page 22.

5. can for ability

The lesson uses can only in ordinary phrases such as can help you and can you tell me; the focus of this lesson is advice, not ability.
