Grammar 1 — the present simple

I / you / we / they

  • I drink milk in the morning.
  • I don’t drink milk in the morning.
  • Do you drink milk in the morning?

He / she / it

  • He drinks milk in the morning.
  • He doesn’t drink milk in the morning.
  • Does he drink milk in the morning?

This is the Let’s Learn box printed on Pupil’s Book page 11. With he, she and it, add -s to the verb and use doesn’t for the negative. In a question, the helping verb (Do or Does) comes first and the main verb has no ending: Does she play basketball?

Grammar 2 — adding -s, -es and -ies

Spelling ruleExamples from the lesson
Most verbs: add -stake → takes · wake → wakes · help → helps · start → starts · read → reads · sleep → sleeps · play → plays
Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -o: add -eswatch → watches · go → goes · do → does
have becomes hasToni always has dinner. · She has breakfast.
After don’t or doesn’t, use the plain verbShe doesn’t set the table. · He doesn’t drink milk.

Useful classroom language

What do you usually do in the morning? — This question asks about a regular morning action.

What do you always do in the evening? — The same question with a different adverb, so the answer must use that adverb: I always go to bed early.

What time do you go to school? — This asks for a time, and the answer gives one: I go to school at twenty to eight.

What do you do in your day? — This is the question at the end of Emma’s text on Workbook page 13; the passage prints it as “What does you do in your day?” with the mistake underlined.
