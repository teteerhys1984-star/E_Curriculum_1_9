Grammar 1 — Present Simple

Used for: facts which are always true; habits or things we do regularly.

Examples printed in this lesson's grammar work: I went to the library yesterday (past simple); Ahmad was watching T.V. (past progressive).

We use the present simple for facts that are always true and for habits or repeated actions. With he, she, and it, add -s to the verb (for example, He drinks). Common frequency adverbs: always, usually, sometimes, never, every day.

Grammar 2 — Present Progressive

Used for: activities happening at the moment of speaking; temporary activities.

Examples printed: Ahmad was watching T.V. when his friend came to visit him (past progressive); I was studying when the electricity went out (past progressive).

We use the present progressive for actions happening now and temporary activities: am/is/are + verb-ing.

Grammar 3 — Past Simple and Past Progressive (Workbook page 8)

Past Simple: used to describe an action that happened in the recent past or the distant past. Action duration is not important. Example: I went to the library yesterday.

Past Progressive: used to describe an action that was in progress at a specific time in the past. Example: Ahmad was watching T.V. when his friend came to visit him.

Use the past progressive for the longer background action (“while we were playing football”) and the past simple for the shorter interrupting event (“I broke my arm”).

Grammar 4 — Future (will / going to)

With will: to make decisions, promises, predictions; to state a fact about the future. With going to: to talk about an event planned before the time of speaking; to make a future prediction based on facts or evidence.

Examples: We will go to the theater tomorrow. They are going to spend their vacation in Spain next year.

The future-tense rules are taught in the curriculum's Workbook tense-reference tables and are summarised here for review.
